# Reactions in Metabolism {#metabolism}

Metabolism looks complicated because there are so many steps, but almost every step is one of the reaction types on this page. When you meet metabolism later in the course, name the type of each step before trying to memorize it.

## One pattern, used again and again {#pattern}

**Oxidation (FAD), addition of water, oxidation (NAD⁺).** A –CH₂–CH₂– becomes a C=C, water adds to make an alcohol, and the alcohol is oxidized to a ketone. Your cells use the **same three-step pattern** to break down fatty acids, two carbons at a time. Recognize it once and you'll see it in both places.

## Reading an enzyme's name {#enzyme-names}

Enzyme names end in **-ase**, and the rest of the name usually tells you the reaction type:

| Name contains | Reaction type | Examples on this page |
|---|---|---|
| dehydrogenase, oxidase | **oxidation** | alcohol dehydrogenase, succinate dehydrogenase, glucose oxidase |
| reductase | **reduction** | aldose reductase |
| hydratase, dehydratase, or a name like fumar*ase* | **addition or elimination** of water | fumarase, aconitase |
| a bond or nutrient + -ase | **hydrolysis** | lipase, peptidase, lactase, maltase |
| synthetase, ligase | **condensation**, using ATP | the enzymes that build fats, proteins, and glycogen |

Biochemists sort all enzymes into **classes** by the type of reaction they run. Three of the classes are the three pairs on this page:

| Enzyme class | What it does | Reaction pair |
|---|---|---|
| **Oxidoreductases** | remove or add H (using NAD⁺, FAD, or NADPH) | oxidation and reduction |
| **Lyases** | add pieces across a double bond, or remove them to form one, without splitting the molecule with water | addition and elimination |
| **Hydrolases** | split a bond with water | hydrolysis |
| **Ligases** | join two molecules, using energy from ATP | condensation |
| **Transferases** | move a group, such as a phosphate or an amino group, from one molecule to another | — |
| **Isomerases** | rearrange atoms within one molecule | — |

<div class="health" markdown="1">
**Health connection: enzymes on a blood panel.** Damaged cells leak their enzymes into the blood, so enzyme levels are used to diagnose disease. A liver panel measures **ALT** and **AST**, two transferases that move amino groups; high levels point to liver damage. **LDH**, the [lactate dehydrogenase](#reduction) above, rises with damage to many tissues.
</div>

## Combustion: the whole trip at once {#combustion}

Burning glucose takes it all the way to the most oxidized form of carbon, CO₂, in one step, releasing its energy as heat:

[[rxn r_comb]]

Your cells reach exactly the same products, with the same overall equation, but in about twenty small steps: oxidations that hand the hydrogens to NAD⁺ and FAD, plus additions, eliminations, and hydrolyses along the way. Breaking it into small steps lets the cell capture the energy as ATP instead of losing it as heat.

## Where you'll see these next

- **Proteins and enzymes:** peptide bonds (condensation and hydrolysis), how enzymes speed up these reactions, and enzymes used in diagnosis.
- **Carbohydrates and lipids:** sugar rings (addition), glycosidic bonds and triglycerides (condensation), and their digestion (hydrolysis).
- **Metabolism:** NAD⁺ and FAD as coenzymes; glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and fatty acid breakdown, where nearly every step is an oxidation, an addition, an elimination, or a hydrolysis.
