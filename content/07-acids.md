# Carboxylic Acids, Esters, and Anhydrides {#acids}

These are the top of the priority ladder. Each contains a C=O with an oxygen attached to the same carbon.

## Carboxylic acids: –COOH

- Replace the parent's final **-e** with **-oic acid**: *butanoic acid*.
- The COOH carbon is **always C1**, so no locant is needed for the suffix.
- When a COOH is attached to a **ring**, the COOH carbon isn't part of the ring: add **-carboxylic acid** to the ring's name.
- Several small acids are best known by common names, and **formic acid** and **acetic acid** are the preferred names under the current rules.

| Carbons | Systematic name | Common name | Where you'll find it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | methanoic acid | **formic acid** | ant stings |
| 2 | ethanoic acid | **acetic acid** | vinegar |
| 3 | propanoic acid | propionic acid | food preservative |
| 4 | butanoic acid | butyric acid | rancid butter |

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it: 2,2-dimethyl-3,4-dioxobutanoic acid</summary>

1. **Highest-priority group:** the carboxylic acid, so the suffix is **-oic acid** and the COOH carbon is C1.
2. **Parent:** four carbons including C1, so **butanoic acid**.
3. **Prefixes:** two methyls on C2 (**2,2-dimethyl**); a ketone C=O on C3 and an aldehyde C=O on C4, both lower priority, so both become **oxo**: **3,4-dioxo**.
4. **Alphabetize:** **d**imethyl and **d**ioxo both start with *d*, but multiplying prefixes don't count, so compare **m**ethyl and **o**xo: methyl first.
5. **Name:** **2,2-dimethyl-3,4-dioxobutanoic acid**.
</details>

<div class="health" markdown="1">
**Health connection.** 2-Amino-3-phenylpropanoic acid is **phenylalanine**, one of the 20 amino acids in proteins. People with phenylketonuria (PKU) can't break it down, which is why diet sodas carry the warning "Phenylketonurics: contains phenylalanine." Every newborn in the US is screened for PKU.
</div>

## Carboxylate salts

Removing the acidic H from a carboxylic acid gives a **carboxylate** anion. Name the salt like an ionic compound: the **metal or cation first**, then the acid name with **-oic acid** changed to **-oate** (or *-ic acid* to *-ate* for common names).

[[figs salt1 salt3 salt2 salt4]]

<div class="health" markdown="1">
**Health connection.** At the pH of your blood (about 7.4), most carboxylic acids exist as carboxylate ions. That's why biochemistry names often use the salt form: **lactate**, **pyruvate**, and **citrate** rather than lactic, pyruvic, and citric acid.
</div>

## Esters: C–CO–O–C

An ester is a carboxylic acid with its acidic H replaced by a carbon group. Name it in two words, like a salt:

1. **First word:** the carbon group attached to the single-bonded oxygen, as an alkyl group: *methyl*, *ethyl*, *propan-2-yl*.
2. **Second word:** the acid part, with **-oic acid** changed to **-oate**: *ethanoate*, or *acetate* from the common name.

[[figs est1 est2 est3 est5]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it: finding the two halves of an ester</summary>

1. **Find the ester oxygen:** the O with single bonds to two carbons, next to a C=O.
2. **Split the molecule there.** The side **with the C=O** is the acid part; the other side is the alkyl group.
3. **Name the alkyl group first:** in propan-2-yl 2,2-dimethylpropanoate, the group on oxygen is attached by its middle carbon, so it's **propan-2-yl** (isopropyl).
4. **Name the acid part as -oate:** three carbons counting the C=O carbon, with two methyls on C2: **2,2-dimethylpropanoate**.
</details>

<div class="health" markdown="1">
**Health connection.** Esters give fruits their smells: ethyl acetate is in nail polish remover and fruity flavors. **Aspirin** is an ester of salicylic acid; it's also still a carboxylic acid, so it's named as an acid with an *acetyloxy* prefix. **Benzocaine**, the topical anesthetic in dental gels like Orajel, is an ethyl ester. The **triglycerides** in your blood are esters of glycerol and three fatty acids.
</div>

## Anhydrides: C–CO–O–CO–C

An anhydride forms when two carboxylic acids join and lose a water molecule. Name it by changing **acid** to **anhydride**: two acetic acids give **acetic anhydride**.

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**Why anhydrides matter in biochemistry.** The same "acid + acid − water" bond forms between **phosphoric acid** units. These **phosphoanhydride** bonds hold the phosphates of **ATP** together, and breaking them powers your cells. See [Biomolecule Connections](#bio).
</div>
