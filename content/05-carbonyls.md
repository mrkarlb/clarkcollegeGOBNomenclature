# Aldehydes and Ketones {#carbonyls}

Both contain a **carbonyl group**, C=O. Where the carbonyl sits decides the family: at the **end** of a chain, it's an **aldehyde**; **inside** the chain, with carbon on both sides, it's a **ketone**. Aldehydes rank just above ketones on the ladder.

## Aldehydes: –CHO

- Replace the parent's final **-e** with **-al**.
- The aldehyde carbon is **always C1**, so no locant is needed for *-al*.
- When a CHO group is attached to a **ring**, the CHO carbon isn't part of the ring. Add **-carbaldehyde** to the ring's name: *cyclohexanecarbaldehyde*.
- As a prefix, the C=O of an aldehyde on a chain is **oxo-**; a CHO group on a ring is **formyl-**.

[[figs ald1 ald2 ald3 ald4 ald5]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it: 4-oxopentanal</summary>

1. **Highest-priority group:** this molecule has an aldehyde and a ketone. The aldehyde ranks higher, so it takes the suffix **-al**, and its carbon is C1.
2. **Parent:** five carbons, **pentanal**.
3. **The ketone becomes a prefix:** the C=O on C4 is **4-oxo-**.
4. **Name:** **4-oxopentanal**.
</details>

## Ketones: C–CO–C

- Replace the parent's final **-e** with **-one**, and number the chain so the carbonyl carbon gets the lowest number: *butan-2-one*.
- In a ring, the carbonyl carbon is C1: *cyclohexan-1-one*. Number around the ring to give any double bond or branch the lowest number.
- As a prefix, a ketone C=O is **oxo-**.

[[figs ket1 ket2 ket3 ket4 ket5]]

<details class="walk" markdown="1">
<summary>Walk through it: 4-(dimethylamino)pent-4-en-2-one</summary>

1. **Highest-priority group:** the ketone outranks the amine, so the suffix is **-one** and the amine nitrogen becomes a prefix.
2. **Parent:** the five-carbon chain holding the C=O. Number from the end nearest it: C2. The double bond is between C4 and C5: **pent-4-en-2-one**.
3. **The nitrogen group:** an N carrying two methyl groups, attached to C4, is a **dimethylamino** prefix. Parentheses keep the complex prefix together.
4. **Name:** **4-(dimethylamino)pent-4-en-2-one**.
</details>

<div class="health" markdown="1">
**Health connection.** Propan-2-one is **acetone**, a common solvent and one of the **ketone bodies** your liver makes when it burns fat instead of glucose. In uncontrolled diabetes, ketone bodies build up in the blood (diabetic ketoacidosis), and acetone can be smelled on the breath. **Formaldehyde** (methanal) preserves tissue specimens.
</div>
